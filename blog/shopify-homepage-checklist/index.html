SHOPIFY HOMEPAGE GUIDE

Shopify Homepage Checklist What You Need Before Launch

Review messaging, navigation, merchandising, trust, mobile usability, SEO, performance, and QA before your Shopify homepage goes live.

QUICK ANSWER

A homepage needs to perform a few important jobs well

Before launch, clearly communicate what you sell, who it is for, and where customers should go next. Review navigation, value proposition, calls to action, collections, products, genuine trust information, mobile usability, footer navigation, metadata, alt text, links, performance, and analytics.

Do not delay launch because the homepage lacks every possible marketing section. Prioritize what helps customers understand, navigate, evaluate, and begin shopping.

HOMEPAGE PURPOSE

The ORRC homepage framework

Orient

Tell visitors what the business sells, who it serves, and why it may be relevant.

Route

Give obvious paths into important collections, categories, products, and search.

Reassure

Provide enough credible information about the offer and business to keep shopping.

Convert

Move visitors toward browsing a collection, viewing a product, or another intentional action.

Orient → Route → Reassure → Convert

Every major homepage section should support at least one of these functions.

LAUNCH CHECKLIST

What your Shopify homepage should include

1. Clear header and navigation

Use customer language, prioritize major shopping paths, make search and cart easy to find, and avoid unnecessary menu levels.

2. Recognizable brand and logo

Use a clear, appropriately sized logo with consistent colors and typography.

3. Primary value proposition

Explain what you sell, who it is for, and the most important reason to continue in concise language.

4. Main call to action

Use specific labels such as Shop New Arrivals or Explore Collections and link to the correct destination.

5. Important collections

Route customers around how they shop—category, use case, room, material, compatibility, or other meaningful intent.

6. Featured products where useful

Use real products, correct pricing, availability, images, and links. Do not feature items merely to fill space.

7. Product benefits

Communicate verifiable value propositions without vague claims or repetitive icon rows.

8. Trust information

Surface accurate shipping, returns, guarantees, payment, sourcing, or service information where it reduces uncertainty.

9. Social proof when genuine

Use reviews, testimonials, press, or customer imagery only when authentic and relevant.

10. Brand story

Include a concise story when origin, expertise, mission, or people strengthen the purchase decision.

11. Educational content

Use buying guides, care content, comparisons, or articles when they help customers choose.

12. Email signup

Offer a clear reason to subscribe and confirm the form, consent language, and follow-up work.

13. Complete footer

Link contact, shipping, returns, policies, account, and other important resources without hiding support.

RESPONSIVE EXPERIENCE

Mobile usability and performance

Mobile checks

  • Header and menu work with touch
  • Headlines wrap cleanly
  • Buttons remain visible and tappable
  • Images crop intentionally
  • Carousels, popups, and sticky controls do not overlap
  • Collection and product routes remain obvious

Performance checks

  • Compress appropriately sized media
  • Avoid unnecessary autoplay video
  • Review apps and embeds
  • Reduce low-value animations
  • Test on representative devices and connections
  • Confirm analytics without duplicate scripts

DISCOVERABILITY AND QA

SEO, accessibility, and technical checks

  • Set a descriptive homepage title and useful meta description.
  • Add an intentional social-sharing image.
  • Use meaningful image alt text where images convey information.
  • Keep important collection links crawlable.
  • Use one clear page heading and logical section headings.
  • Remove placeholder copy, links, products, and metadata.
  • Test every navigation item, CTA, form, product route, and footer link.
  • Follow the complete path from homepage to a successful test order.

AVOID CLUTTER

Common homepage mistakes

  1. Trying to say everything above the fold.
  2. Using generic theme copy instead of a clear offer.
  3. Adding sections because the theme provides them.
  4. Showing too many competing calls to action.
  5. Using unverified trust claims or placeholder reviews.
  6. Linking buttons to the wrong collections or empty pages.
  7. Designing desktop first and testing mobile last.
  8. Adding apps and media without reviewing performance.
  9. Polishing the homepage while cart and checkout remain untested.

FAQ

Shopify homepage FAQ

What should a Shopify homepage include?

A clear offer, useful navigation, primary CTA, important shopping paths, relevant products or collections, credible trust information, and complete footer links.

How long should a Shopify homepage be?

There is no ideal length. Include enough information to orient, route, reassure, and convert without repeating messages or delaying the shopping journey.

Do I need reviews on the homepage?

No. Use reviews only when genuine and helpful. Other verified trust information may be more appropriate for a new store.

How do I optimize a homepage for SEO?

Use a descriptive title, useful metadata, clear navigation, crawlable links, accurate content, relevant images, and good technical performance.

What should I test before publishing?

Test navigation, links, product destinations, forms, mobile layouts, images, apps, promotions, footer links, and the complete homepage-to-checkout path.
