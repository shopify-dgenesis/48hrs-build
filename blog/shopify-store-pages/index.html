What Pages Does a Shopify Store Need? Complete Launch Guide

Build the pages customers need to discover products, make confident decisions, complete checkout, and get support—without wasting launch time on arbitrary page counts.

QUICK ANSWER

Build around customer decisions, not page count

Most Shopify stores need a homepage, collection or catalog paths, product pages, cart and checkout, contact information, relevant policies, and enough shipping, returns, and business information for customers to purchase confidently.

About, FAQ, size guides, wholesale, subscriptions, store locator, comparison pages, and blogs depend on the business. Shopify also manages templates and commerce experiences that are not ordinary manually created pages.

PAGE ARCHITECTURE

The four types of Shopify store pages

Commerce pages

Homepage, collections, products, search, cart, and checkout support discovery and purchasing.

Trust and support pages

Contact, About, FAQ, shipping, returns, sizing, and product care resolve customer questions.

Policy and compliance pages

Privacy, refunds, shipping, terms, legal notices, and subscription policies communicate important obligations.

Growth pages

Blogs, wholesale, loyalty, store locators, gift cards, buying guides, landing pages, press, and careers should serve a real commercial purpose.

LAUNCH FOUNDATION

Essential Shopify pages and experiences

Homepage

Explain the offer, establish trust, and route customers into products and collections. It should orient—not contain every detail.

Collection and category pages

Group products around shopping intent with useful titles, descriptions, merchandising, filters, and clear paths to product pages.

Product pages

Help customers understand the item, evaluate benefits and specifications, choose variants, see pricing and availability, and purchase confidently.

Search results

Important for broader catalogs and shoppers who know what they want. Test useful queries, misspellings, and no-result states.

Cart

Confirm products, variants, quantities, discounts, estimates, and the route to checkout without creating unnecessary friction.

Checkout

Shopify manages checkout, but merchants must configure payments, shipping, taxes, customer details, branding, and test orders.

Contact and support

Give customers an obvious way to reach the business and set expectations for channels and response times.

Shipping and returns

Explain destinations, methods, timing, costs, thresholds, return windows, conditions, exchanges, and refund handling.

Policies

Use relevant privacy, terms, refund, shipping, legal, or subscription policies and make important information accessible before checkout.

BUSINESS-DEPENDENT

Pages you may need

About

Useful where origin, expertise, mission, sourcing, or people strengthen trust and differentiation.

FAQ

Create one for recurring store-wide questions. Keep product-specific answers close to the relevant product decision.

Size or specification guide

Needed when measurements, compatibility, fit, dimensions, or technical requirements affect variant selection.

Product care

Useful for washing, maintenance, storage, installation, safety, or warranty information.

Accounts and subscriptions

Explain account benefits, recurring orders, cancellation, billing, and management when these models apply.

Wholesale and store locator

Add only when customers or trade buyers genuinely need these purchasing paths.

NAVIGATION AND SEO

Connect pages logically

Keep navigation aligned with customer language, use crawlable links, and avoid burying important pages. Product-specific answers belong on product pages; delivery expectations belong near product, cart, or shipping information; legal details belong in policies.

A blog is optional. Publish editorial content only when it genuinely helps customers or supports product discovery. Strong product and collection architecture remains essential with or without one.

COMMON MISTAKES

Avoid unnecessary or incomplete pages

  1. Building pages to reach an arbitrary number.
  2. Trying to place every message on the homepage.
  3. Using generic policy text without business review.
  4. Hiding shipping and return information until checkout.
  5. Creating a giant FAQ instead of improving product pages.
  6. Publishing empty or poorly organized collections.
  7. Using placeholders that do not reflect final content.
  8. Adding custom code where theme sections can manage content.

FAQ

Shopify store pages FAQ

How many pages should a Shopify store have?

There is no ideal number. Prioritize a complete customer journey based on your catalog, products, questions, policies, markets, and business model.

What are the most important Shopify pages?

Homepage, collections, products, cart, checkout, contact/support, shipping, returns, and relevant policies form the foundation for most stores.

Does every Shopify store need an About page?

No, but it is valuable when the brand story, expertise, origin, sourcing, or people help customers trust and choose the business.

Does every store need an FAQ?

No. Create one when recurring store-wide questions benefit from centralization; keep contextual answers where decisions happen.

Where should policies appear?

Important policies should be easy to reach from storefront navigation, commonly the footer, and surfaced closer to product or cart decisions when relevant.

Does my Shopify store need a blog for SEO?

No. A blog is optional. Useful product information, collection architecture, internal links, crawlability, and technically sound pages matter regardless.
